# Cover Letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **"Survey effort moderates the climatic
visibility of new bird distribution records across China,"** for publication as an
Article/Letter.

New species records are among the most widely used signals of climate-driven
range change, yet a record is produced only when ecological exposure and human
observation coincide. The field almost universally corrects for survey effort by
adding it as a covariate or an offset — treatments that assume effort scales
record probability independently of ecological exposure. We show this assumption
is wrong for new distribution records, and that the correction itself is a
testable hypothesis.

Using 12,813 species-by-province-by-year risk-set rows for 333 birds reported as
new records across mainland China (2002–2024), we fit discrete-time hazard models
and find that climate exposure and effort combine **interactively, not
additively** (hazard ratio 1.18–1.30 across four effort metrics; all
p ≤ 6 × 10⁻⁵). The central result is a clean discriminating test: an interaction
model decisively defeats a raw-effort offset model in seven of eight comparisons,
identifying effort as a **moderator of climatic visibility** rather than a scaling
factor on records. The finding survives a fifteen-fold expansion of the risk set
(province p = 1 × 10⁻¹⁴), attenuates without reversing across administrative
scales, and gives way to effort-density discovery beyond a well-modelled species
core — an interpretable robustness boundary rather than a failure.

Two contributions travel beyond birds and China. First, a reframing of new
distribution records as **effort-moderated visibility events**, with direct
consequences for how range-shift inference should treat observation effort.
Second, a reusable **interaction-versus-offset discriminator** that any
effort-aware study can run to check whether its effort correction is adequate. We
also translate the result into monitoring practice, recommending prioritisation on
the product of climatic exposure and current effort gap rather than on projected
hazard alone.

We have held the manuscript to a strict reproducibility standard: every reported
number is traced to a persisted result table, and analyses that are implemented
but not yet finalised (ecological-grid refits, spatial-block cross-validation,
lagged-effort identification) are stated as roadmap items rather than reported
with numbers. We believe this honesty about the evidence boundary strengthens,
rather than weakens, the contribution.

The work is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and the author declares
no competing interests. We suggest reviewers with expertise in macroecology and
species distributions, statistical/hierarchical ecology, and citizen-science
monitoring design.

Thank you for considering our submission.
